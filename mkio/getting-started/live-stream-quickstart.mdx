This quickstart takes you from a blank project to a working live stream. You will create a live event, configure a streaming endpoint and locator, start your encoder, and copy a playback URL. The entire flow takes about five minutes if your encoder is already installed.

## Before you begin

Make sure you have:

- An MK.IO account with at least one project. See [Account Setup](/mkio/getting-started/account-setup).
- At least one storage account connected to your project. See [Connect Cloud Storage](/mkio/getting-started/connect-cloud-storage).
- An encoder installed — [OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com), FFmpeg, or GStreamer all work. OBS is the easiest starting point.

## Create and start a live stream

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a live event">
    In your MK.IO project, go to **Live Events** in the left navigation and click **+ Create Live Event**.

    Fill in the basic settings:

    - **Name**: A unique identifier for this event (for example, `my-first-stream`). Names cannot be reused within a project once a live event is deleted.
    - **Description**: Optional.

    **Choose an event type:**

    | Mode | What MK.IO does | Best for |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Live passthrough (up to 5 Mbps) | Passes the stream through unchanged | Low-bandwidth streams encoded at source |
    | Live passthrough (up to 60 Mbps) | Passthrough with a higher bandwidth ceiling | High-bitrate professional sources |
    | **Live encoding (720p)** | Produces adaptive bitrate output at 720p and lower | Most use cases — recommended default |
    | Live encoding (1080p) | Produces ABR output up to 1080p | High-quality productions |

    Select **Live encoding (720p)** if you are unsure. It lets MK.IO generate multiple bitrate levels automatically so viewers on different connections all get a smooth stream.

    **Select the input protocol:**

    - **RTMP**: Supported by OBS, Wirecast, and most hardware encoders. Use this for your first stream.
    - **RTMPS**: Encrypted RTMP for networks that require encrypted ingest.
    - **SRT**: Better reliability over unpredictable networks. Requires live encoding mode.

    **Configure output settings:**

    - **Archive window length**: How long MK.IO retains the live recording. Set this to at least 1 hour for a typical event. The full recording is stored as an asset when you stop the event.
    - **Storage account**: Select the connected storage account where the recording will be saved.

    Click **Review**, then **Submit** to create the live event.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create a streaming endpoint">
    A streaming endpoint is the delivery server that packages your content as HLS and DASH and serves viewer requests. Go to **Streaming Endpoints** in the left navigation and click **+ Create Streaming Endpoint**.

    - **Name**: Use something like `default` or `production`.
    - **Type**: Select **Shared** for testing. Shared endpoints support up to 600 Mbps on shared infrastructure.
    - **Auto-start**: Enable this to start the endpoint immediately after creation.

    Click **Create**.

    <Warning>A running streaming endpoint incurs costs even when no content is actively being played. Stop the endpoint when you finish testing.</Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create a streaming locator">
    A streaming locator attaches to your live output asset and generates the playback URL paths. While the live event is still in the **Stopped** state, you can set this up in advance.

    Go to **Assets** and find the asset that MK.IO created for your live output (it shares the name you gave the live event). Click the asset name, then scroll to **Streaming Locators** and click **+ Add Streaming Locator**.

    - **Name**: A unique name (for example, `live-stream-access`).
    - **Streaming policy**: Select `Predefined_ClearStreamingOnly` for an unprotected stream.
    - Leave **Content key policy** empty for clear streaming.

    Click **Add**.

    <Note>If you want DRM protection on your stream, select a DRM streaming policy and attach a content key policy. See [Content Protection](/mkio/concepts/content-protection).</Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Start the live event">
    Go back to **Live Events** and click your event name. Click **Start**.

    MK.IO provisions the ingest infrastructure and generates an **Input URL**. Copy this URL — you will paste it into your encoder.

    The event takes a few seconds to transition to the **Running** state before it is ready to accept an ingest connection.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure your encoder">
    Open OBS Studio (or your encoder of choice) and point it at the MK.IO ingest URL.

    **In OBS Studio:**

    1. Go to **Settings** > **Stream**.
    2. Set **Service** to **Custom**.
    3. Paste the MK.IO **Input URL** into the **Server** field.
    4. Leave the **Stream Key** field empty (the key is included in the URL).
    5. Click **OK**, then click **Start Streaming** in the main OBS window.

    Once OBS connects, MK.IO begins receiving and encoding your stream.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Get your playback URL">
    Go back to your asset in MK.IO. Open the **Streaming Endpoint** dropdown and select your running endpoint. Open the **Streaming locator** dropdown and select the locator you created. Click **Apply**.

    MK.IO generates your HLS and DASH playback URLs:

    ```
    https://<endpoint-name>.<region>.streaming.mediakind.com/<locator-id>/<manifest>.ism/manifest(format=m3u8-cmaf)
    https://<endpoint-name>.<region>.streaming.mediakind.com/<locator-id>/<manifest>.ism/manifest(format=mpd-time-cmaf)
    ```

    Paste either URL into a media player — the built-in MKPlayer on the asset page, VLC, or any HLS/DASH-compatible player — to verify the stream is playing.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Stop the event when finished">
    When your broadcast ends, go to **Live Events** and click **Stop** on the live event. Then go to **Streaming Endpoints** and stop the endpoint.

    Both resources incur costs while running. If you do not need the recording, also delete the output asset to avoid ongoing storage charges.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connection timeout

If MK.IO receives no data on an RTMP or RTMPS connection for 10 seconds, it drops the connection automatically. This timeout is fixed and cannot be configured. If your stream stops unexpectedly, check that your encoder or network did not pause for more than 10 seconds. Your encoder must reconnect and resume sending data to restore the stream.
